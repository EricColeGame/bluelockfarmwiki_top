## Why Blue Lock Farm Abilities Decide How Fast You Earn

Blue Lock Farm abilities are the difference between a base that limps along at a few thousand Cash per minute and one that keeps earning while you stand in the Plaza doing nothing. If you've been buying lockers and hatching players but your crate sales still feel small, the bottleneck is rarely the players themselves — it's the modifier systems layered on top of them. That's why blue lock farm abilities deserve more attention than any single code you redeem: codes hand you tokens, but abilities decide what those tokens are actually worth.

The base loop itself is simple. Buy lockers, hatch players, let those players farm soccer balls, then sell full crates for Cash. Every dollar you earn goes back into better lockers and upgrades. Abilities are everything that changes the numbers inside that loop — how fast it runs, how much each crate sells for, and how good your next hatch is likely to be.

| Layer | What it changes | Currency | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Traits | How an individual hatched player performs | Trait Tokens | Code rewards and progression |
| Grades | The Cash multiplier attached to a player | Grade Tokens or Cash | Ranking stand in the Plaza |
| Potions | Temporary boosts to Cash, luck, and grade speed | Free items | Codes and rewards |
| Upgrades | Base-wide stats (crate value, luck, opening speed, movement, base size) | Cash | Selling crates |

Notice the pattern: three of the four layers are fed by things you earn for free, and only one — permanent upgrades — is fed directly by Cash. That's the entire strategy in one sentence. Use free tokens to raise your ceiling, then pour Cash into the upgrades that raise your floor.

### How the Layers Compound

None of the four layers of blue lock farm abilities work in isolation. A high grade on a player with no supporting upgrades still produces modest crates. Great upgrades with ungraded players hit a ceiling fast. The players who progress quickest are the ones who raise every layer at once instead of dumping everything into a single system.

## Traits and Grade Tokens: The Two Token Systems

Traits and grades are the two systems most players confuse, because both use tokens and both make individual players stronger. They do very different jobs, though, and mixing them up is an easy way to waste a stack of rewards.

### Traits: The Layer That Shapes Individual Players

Traits are the layer that shapes a specific hatched player rather than your whole base. The game hands out Trait Tokens in bulk through its newest codes — one drops 25, another drops 20 alongside Grade Tokens and a Variant Token — which tells you the developers treat traits as a core progression track rather than a rare luxury.

Based on player experience, trait tokens are usually best concentrated rather than spread. Dropping a full stack on one player you plan to keep tends to produce more noticeable results than sprinkling a few tokens across every hatch in your base. Because Trait Tokens are the most common code reward, you can afford to experiment early and then commit once you know which players are staying in your lineup.

### Grades: The Biggest Multiplier in the Game

Grade Tokens do one job: they grade players. You spend them at the Ranking stand in the Plaza, and grading raises the Cash multiplier that player produces. Cash can also be used to roll rankings, but for high-tier units those rolls can run into the millions per attempt — which makes Grade Tokens the efficient route for anyone still building a base. The UR grade carries the largest Cash multiplier available, so it's the long-term target for players you intend to keep.

| Token | What it feeds | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trait Token | The trait layer on your players | Code rewards (the largest amounts come from the newest codes) |
| Grade Token | Grading players at the Ranking stand | Code rewards |
| Variant Token | A variant-related reward; its exact role is still being mapped by the community | Rare code drops |
| Jackpot Token | A chance at a bonus reward | Code rewards |

| Grading method | Cost | Best for | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grade Tokens | Free from codes and rewards | Your best long-term players | Limited supply |
| Cash rolls | Can reach millions per roll on high-tier units | Topping up once tokens run dry | Competes directly with your upgrade budget |

The practical rule: never spend Cash on a ranking roll while you still have Grade Tokens sitting in your inventory. Save the paid rolls for the moment your tokens hit zero and you're one grade away from a UR.

## Potions: Timed Boosts That Multiply Your Farm

Potions are the most misunderstood of the blue lock farm abilities because they don't change anything permanently — they change how much you get out of a short window. That makes timing more important than quantity. A code that grants three Cash Potions is worth far more if you pop them right before you dump a full set of crates than if you use them the second you redeem the code.

| Potion | What it boosts | Best moment to use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash Potion | Cash earned from your farm | Just before a large crate sell-off |
| Grade Speed Potion | How quickly grading progresses | Before a grading session at the Ranking stand |
| Luck Potion | Luck on hatches and rolls | Immediately before a hatching spree |

Two habits separate players who get value from potions and players who waste them:

- **Pair the buff with the resource.** A Grade Speed Potion is dead weight if you have no Grade Tokens or Cash to grade with. Stock up first, then activate.
- **Stack sessions, not minutes.** Bank your potions, then run one long hatching or selling session with all of them active instead of dripping them out one at a time.

Community reports also suggest saving Luck Potions for moments when you're hatching with your best lockers, since luck multiplies the odds on the hatches that matter most.

## Permanent Upgrades: Where Cash Becomes Power

The permanent upgrade track is where blue lock farm abilities stop being temporary and start compounding. These upgrades apply to your whole base, and they're the only layer you buy with the Cash your players generate. There are five tracks worth tracking: crate value, luck, opening speed, movement, and base size.

| Upgrade | What it changes | Suggested priority | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crate value | Cash earned per crate sold | 1 | Direct income — every other upgrade is measured against it |
| Opening speed | How fast lockers and crates cycle | 2 | More cycles per hour means more chances at everything |
| Luck | Odds on hatches and rolls | 3 | Improves quality once your quantity is steady |
| Movement | Player speed around the field | 4 | Cuts the downtime between ball collections |
| Base size | How much you can fit in your base | 5 (move up if you're expanding) | Extra space only helps if you can fill it |

This priority order reflects what most players settle into, but it isn't a hard rule. If you're actively expanding your base, base size jumps up the list. If you're spending your sessions waiting on crates to finish, opening speed deserves the next chunk of Cash instead.

A simple rebalancing habit keeps you from over-investing in one track: at the end of each session, ask what actually limited your earnings. Waiting on crates means opening speed. Crates selling cheaply means crate value. Players standing around idle means movement. Follow the bottleneck, not a fixed checklist.

## FAQ

**What are the best blue lock farm abilities to invest in first?**
Start with crate value and opening speed, since those two upgrades directly control how much Cash your farm produces per hour. Once your income is steady, move your free Grade Tokens onto the players you plan to keep, then layer traits on top of them. Potions should be saved for your biggest hatching and selling sessions.

**Should I use Grade Tokens or Cash to grade my players?**
Grade Tokens first, always. They're free from codes and rewards, while high-tier Cash rolls can cost millions per attempt. Save Cash rolls for the moment your tokens run out and you're pushing a keeper toward the UR grade and its largest Cash multiplier.

**Do potions permanently improve my players?**
No. Potions are temporary buffs to Cash, grade speed, or luck. They don't change a player's stats or your base — they only increase what you get out of a short window, which is why timing them around a big session matters so much.

**How do I get more Trait Tokens and Grade Tokens?**
Redeem every active code, then watch the game's Discord server and the Finding Illusions Roblox group for new drops. Milestone rewards and update releases are when fresh codes tend to appear, and Trait Tokens are usually the most generous reward in any new batch.
