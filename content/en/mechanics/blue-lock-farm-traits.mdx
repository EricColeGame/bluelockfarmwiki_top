## What Are Blue Lock Farm Traits?

Blue Lock Farm is a Roblox farming game with a simple loop: buy lockers, hatch soccer players, let those players farm balls, then sell full crates for Cash and pour the earnings back into better lockers. Blue Lock Farm traits are the modifiers you attach to individual players to change how well that whole machine runs — more Cash per crate, better hatch luck, faster grading, quicker crate openings, or a larger base.

Traits are the reason a common unit can sometimes out-earn a flashy one. If you have ever watched someone with an ordinary roster blow past your income, Blue Lock Farm traits are usually the explanation. Because traits sit on top of your players and stack across your whole roster, their value grows as your farm grows.

The mechanic is deliberately random. You spend Trait Tokens to roll a random trait onto a player, and you can keep rolling to overwrite a weak result with a stronger one. The tokens are the resource; the roll is the gamble.

### Traits vs. Grades vs. Variants: Three Different Systems

Players mix these up constantly, and mixing them up wastes tokens. Here is how the three progression layers compare.

| System | Currency | What It Changes | Where You Use It |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Traits | Trait Tokens | Player-level modifiers: Cash, luck, speed, utility | Trait station / player menu |
| Grades | Grade Tokens or Cash | A player's grade tier, which sets its Cash multiplier | Ranking stand in the Plaza |
| Variants | Variant Tokens | Variant versions of a unit that change its look or rarity | Variant station |

Grades are the blunt multiplier. The UR grade carries the biggest Cash multiplier in the game, and rolling for top tiers with Cash alone costs millions per attempt on high-end units — which is exactly why Grade Tokens are so valuable. Traits are the fine-tuning layer that sits on top of whatever grade you land. Variants are the prestige layer you chase later.

The practical takeaway: grade your best units first, then trait them. Traiting a unit you are about to replace is the single most common way players burn tokens for nothing.

### How Trait Tokens Actually Work

Trait Tokens are the only currency that rolls traits. You pick up small amounts from normal play and much larger amounts from codes, events, and milestone rewards. Every roll consumes tokens whether or not the result is good, so the real skill in the trait system is knowing when to stop.

Two habits separate efficient players from frustrated ones:

- **Never roll on a throwaway unit.** Locker upgrades come fast in the early game, and the player you trait today may be gone tomorrow.
- **Bank tokens for a keeper.** Concentrating rolls on one unit you will use for hours beats spreading a thin stack across ten players.

Community reports also suggest that a "good enough" roll kept early is worth more than a perfect roll chased late, because the tokens you save go straight into lockers and upgrades.

### How to Redeem Codes for Trait Tokens

1. Launch Blue Lock Farm on [Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/).
2. Open the **Shop** menu.
3. Scroll to the bottom of the menu and find the **Enter code here** field.
4. Paste a code and hit Redeem.

Copy and paste rather than typing. A single wrong letter or a stray space makes a code invalid, and there is no way to recover a mistyped code. If a code from another source does not work, assume it expired rather than that you typed it wrong.

### Why Trait Stacking Beats Trait Spreading

The strongest farms do not have one perfect trait on one perfect player. They have several players feeding the same stat at once, because every upgrade you buy later amplifies whatever you already boosted. If your upgrades push crate value, cash traits compound with them. If your upgrades push luck, luck traits do the same.

A common community approach is to pick one stat family, commit three to four players to it, and only then branch out. Spreading a single trait across every category gives you a roster that is mildly good at everything and strong at nothing.

| Progression Stage | What to Roll | What to Ignore |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early | Nothing — bank every token | Anything; lockers matter more |
| Mid | One keeper unit, income or luck family | Speed traits, variant chasing |
| Late | Three to five units across two families | Perfect rolls on bench players |

## How to Roll and Reroll Traits Without Wasting Tokens

Rerolling is where most players lose value. Follow this order and you will keep more of your tokens.

1. **Pick the unit first.** Only roll on a player you plan to keep for several hours of farming.
2. **Open the trait interface** on that player and check the current roll cost.
3. **Check what you already have.** If the existing trait is in a top family for your build, you may already be done.
4. **Roll once and compare.** Decide immediately whether the new trait beats the old one.
5. **Keep or replace**, then reassess. Do not chain rolls on autopilot.
6. **Stop at good enough.** Bank the rest for your next keeper.
7. **Repeat after a locker upgrade**, not before it.

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Traiting units you will replace | Tokens vanish with the unit | Roll only on keepers |
| Chasing a perfect roll | Costs scale faster than returns | Accept "good enough" |
| Ignoring grades entirely | Traits multiply a small base | Grade first, trait second |
| Spreading tokens thin | No stat ever gets amplified | Commit to one or two families |
| Forgetting codes | Free tokens expire unused | Check the list weekly |

## Trait Priority, Timing, and What to Do Next

The order of operations for a new account looks like this: build lockers, sell crates, upgrade crate value and luck, then start grading your best unit, then trait it. Traits come last in the early game because they multiply numbers you have not built yet.

Once your base is stable, shift the balance. Codes like TRAITS and ADMINABUSE give you enough Trait Tokens to make a real roll session, and the Potions from COMMUNITY and WEATHER speed up the grading and luck side of the farm at the same time. Stack those rewards together and you get a jump in income that would otherwise take hours of idle farming.

Finally, treat the trait system as long-term. Grades cap out, variants are cosmetic, but traits are the layer you keep refining as your roster improves. Every new keeper unit is a new set of rolls, and every code drop is free progress toward the next one.

## FAQ: Blue Lock Farm Traits

**What are Blue Lock Farm traits?**

They are random modifiers rolled onto individual players with Trait Tokens. Traits boost things like Cash earned, hatch luck, grade speed, opening speed, movement, or base size, and they stack across your roster.

**How do I get Trait Tokens?**

Redeem codes — TRAITS gives 25 tokens and ADMINABUSE gives 20 plus Grade and Variant Tokens — and pick up smaller amounts from events, milestones, and normal gameplay.

**Should I roll traits or grade my players first?**

Grade first. A grade sets a player's Cash multiplier, and traits multiply that number. Traiting an ungraded unit gives you a smaller return on the same tokens.

**Do traits carry over if I replace a player?**

Plan as if they do not. Roll traits only on units you intend to keep, and bank your tokens for the next keeper rather than experimenting on bench players.
