## The Core Blue Lock Farm Classes You'll Manage

Every Blue Lock Farm class falls into one of five buckets, and each one feeds the next. Here's how they fit together.

### Locker Classes

Lockers are the backbone of the whole loop. Each locker tier hatches players of a certain rarity and at a certain speed, and those players are what actually generate soccer balls. A higher locker class costs more Cash up front but produces players that fill crates noticeably faster, which is why most experienced players treat lockers as the first thing to upgrade.

### Player Grade Classes

Grades are the ranking layer. You take a player to the Ranking stand in the Plaza and roll for a grade, and better grades mean bigger Cash multipliers. Lower grades can be rolled with plain Cash, but as you climb, the price per roll balloons — community reports describe high-tier rolls costing into the millions.

### Token Classes

Tokens are the shortcut currency. Grade Tokens let you roll rankings without spending Cash, Trait Tokens unlock trait bonuses, a Variant Token grants a variant player, and a Jackpot Token gives you a shot at a free reward. They come from codes, milestones, and events.

### Upgrade Classes

Upgrades are the multipliers that apply to your entire farm rather than a single player. Crate value, luck, opening speed, movement speed, and base size all live here. These are easy to overlook because they don't look flashy, but they quietly compound everything else you've built.

### Potion Classes

Potions are timed boosts: Cash Potions, Luck Potions, and Grade Speed Potions. They're best saved for sessions when you're actively grinding rather than idling, since the clock keeps ticking whether you're using them or not.

## Locker and Upgrade Classes: Where Your Cash Should Go

The single most common mistake in Blue Lock Farm is spending Cash on shiny player rolls while the underlying farm is still slow. A rough rule that holds up well: if a locker or upgrade purchase would increase your Cash per minute by more than a new roll would, buy the upgrade first.

| Stage | What to Prioritize | Why | Common Mistake |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First 30 minutes | Cheapest lockers that still produce players | Gets the loop running | Saving for one expensive locker too early |
| Early-mid game | Crate value, then opening speed | Both multiply every player you already own | Chasing new players instead of upgrades |
| Mid game | Luck upgrades and movement speed | More valuable drops and faster crate cycling | Ignoring movement, then walking everywhere |
| Late game | Highest locker class plus base size | Base size caps how much you can farm at once | Expanding base before it can be filled |

Player experience suggests that crate value and opening speed pay for themselves faster than almost anything else in the early game, because they improve the output of players you've already hatched rather than requiring a new purchase to see a benefit.

A practical routine looks like this:

- Sell crates the moment they're full instead of letting them sit.
- Bank roughly a third of every sale for upgrades rather than spending it all.
- Only buy a new locker class when your current lockers are consistently full.
- Recheck your upgrade menu after every big sale — costs scale, but so does your income.

## Grade Classes and Token Classes: Getting the Most From Rolls

Grades are where your farm's ceiling gets set. The UR grade offers the largest Cash multiplier in the game, and the gap between it and the tiers below is significant enough that most players make it their long-term target. The catch is cost: high-tier rolls get expensive fast when you pay with Cash.

That's exactly why the token classes matter so much. Grade Tokens let you roll rankings for free, which means a stack of them is effectively a stack of millions in saved Cash. Trait Tokens work the same way for the trait system, and Variant Tokens give you access to a variant player without the usual grind.

| Token or Grade Item | Where It's Used | What It Does | Best Time to Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grade Tokens | Ranking stand in the Plaza | Rolls player grades without spending Cash | Once Cash rolls get expensive |
| Trait Tokens | Trait system | Unlocks trait bonuses on players | After you've locked in a grade |
| Variant Token | Player hatching | Grants a variant player | When you have a locker class worth using it on |
| Jackpot Token | Jackpot reward pool | Chance at a free bonus reward | Immediately — there's no reason to hoard |
| Cash / Luck / Grade Speed Potions | Active gameplay | Temporary multipliers | During a dedicated grinding session |

One detail worth internalizing: since Grade Tokens bypass the Cash cost entirely, the effective value of a single token grows the further you are into the game. Ten Grade Tokens are worth far more to a mid-game player facing million-Cash rolls than to a beginner rolling cheap grades. If you're close to the point where rolls get pricey, hold your tokens rather than burning them early.

## FAQ

**What exactly are Blue Lock Farm classes?**

Blue Lock Farm classes is the catch-all term for the tiers and categories that structure your progression: locker classes, player grade classes, token classes, upgrade classes, and potion classes. Each one feeds the others, which is why upgrading them in the right order matters more than grinding longer.

**Do Blue Lock Farm classes cost Robux?**

No. Everything in the progression loop — lockers, upgrades, grades, and tokens — can be earned with in-game Cash, free codes, and milestone rewards. Spending Robux may speed things up, but it isn't required to reach high-tier grades.

**How do I unlock better Blue Lock Farm classes faster?**

Redeem every active code, prioritize crate value and opening speed early, and save your Grade Tokens for the point where Cash rolls start costing millions. Stacking Cash and Luck Potions during an active grinding session also squeezes more out of the same amount of playtime.

**Are Blue Lock Farm classes the same for every player?**

The categories are the same, but the best order to upgrade them depends on whether you idle or play actively, and how far into the game you are. Use the priority tables above as a starting point, then adjust based on what your farm actually needs.
