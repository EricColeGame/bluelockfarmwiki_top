If your Cash has flatlined while everyone else seems to be printing it, this Blue Lock Farm progression guide will get you moving again. Blue Lock Farm rewards players who reinvest early and upgrade with purpose — not players who hoard Cash and hope for a lucky hatch. Work through the stages below and you'll always know exactly what your next purchase should be.

## How the Blue Lock Farm Progression Loop Works

Every session runs on the same loop: buy lockers, hatch players, let those players farm balls on your base, then sell full crates for Cash. That Cash goes back into better lockers and upgrades, which makes the next round of crates worth more. The loop never changes — only the size of the numbers does.

What separates fast players from stuck ones is *where* they spend. Someone who dumps every dollar into new lockers ends up with a base full of cheap workers and no multiplier behind them. Someone who balances crate value, luck, and capacity keeps compounding.

| Stage | What you're doing | What it produces | Sign it's working |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Hatch | Buy lockers and open them for players | A roster of ball farmers | Your base is full of active players |
| 2. Farm | Players auto-collect balls | Balls stacking toward full crates | Crates fill faster than you can sell them |
| 3. Sell | Convert full crates into Cash | Steady income | Cash per minute keeps climbing |
| 4. Upgrade | Spend Cash on lockers and stat upgrades | Higher crate value and better odds | Each cycle pays more than the last |
| 5. Refine | Grade players, roll traits, use tokens | Multipliers that stack | Grades climb without Cash rolls |

Community reports consistently point to stage four as the one people rush. Don't. The upgrade layer is where your farm actually scales, and skipping it is the single most common reason players stall out in the mid game.

## Early Game: Lockers, Hatching, and Your First Real Cash

Your first goal is simple: get players on the field and crates selling. Buy the cheapest lockers you can afford, hatch whatever comes out, and let the farm run. Don't agonize over which player you pull early — quantity beats quality until your income is stable enough to be picky.

### Upgrade priority for new players

| Upgrade | What it improves | Priority | Why it matters early |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crate value | Cash earned per crate | Highest | Multiplies every other purchase you make |
| Luck | Odds of pulling stronger players | High | Better players mean a better base without extra lockers |
| Base size | How many players can farm at once | Medium-high | More workers means more balls per minute |
| Movement | How quickly players reach balls | Medium | Smooths the loop so nothing sits idle |
| Opening speed | How fast lockers hatch | Medium | A nice quality-of-life boost while income is small |

A good rule of thumb: if an upgrade increases the value of what you already own, buy it before you buy another locker. New lockers add cost. Upgrades multiply what you've already paid for.

### Don't ignore movement and base size

Movement and base size are the two upgrades new players skip most often. Both feel minor until you watch a base where half the players are standing around doing nothing. If your crates aren't filling fast enough to keep you busy, that's usually a capacity problem, not a luck problem.

## Mid Game: Where Upgrades Multiply Your Crate Value

Once you have a full base and steady crate sales, the game changes. You're no longer buying your first players — you're upgrading the ones you have and chasing better pulls. This is the stretch of a Blue Lock Farm progression guide where most accounts either take off or plateau, and the difference is rarely luck.

Three things matter here:

- **Keep stacking crate value.** Every increase applies to every crate you will ever sell, which makes it the safest purchase in the game.
- **Bank lockers, then open in one sitting.** Popping a Luck Potion before a big opening spree gets far more out of it than trickling lockers out one at a time.
- **Buy lockers that match your income.** A locker tier you can't afford to keep opening is worse than upgrading the tier you already own.

| Situation | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Crates sell fast but Cash barely moves | Crate value is too low | Buy crate value upgrades before new lockers |
| Base looks half empty | Capacity or movement is lagging | Upgrade base size, then movement |
| Plenty of Cash, weak pulls | Luck is the bottleneck | Save lockers and open them with a Luck Potion |
| Income is strong, nothing to spend on | You're ready for the next tier | Start grading your best players |

If you're unsure what to do next, look at whichever number is holding you back — crate value, roster strength, or base capacity — and fix that one first.

## Late Game: Grade Tokens, Traits, and Variants

Grading is where Blue Lock Farm gets expensive. You grade players at the **Ranking stand** in the Plaza, and you have two ways to pay: Cash or Grade Tokens. Cash rolls are fine at low tiers, but high-tier units can cost millions per roll, which makes Cash grading a losing habit once your roster is strong. Grade Tokens let you roll the same table for free.

The UR grade carries the biggest Cash multiplier available, so it's the target most players build toward. Saving Grade Tokens for that push is almost always better than sprinkling them across mid-tier players. The late-game portion of any Blue Lock Farm progression guide really comes down to one habit: bank tokens, then spend them all at once on a player worth keeping.

| Item | What it does | Best time to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Grade Token | Free grade rolls at the Ranking stand | High-tier grading pushes |
| Trait Token | Rerolls a player's traits | On players you plan to keep long-term |
| Variant Token | Grants a variant reward | When you're committing to a keeper |
| Luck Potion | Improves your pull odds | Right before a big locker-opening session |
| Cash Potion | Increases Cash earned | During long, active play sessions |
| Grade Speed Potion | Speeds up the grading process | When you're pushing several grades at once |
| Jackpot Token | Chance at a large free reward | As soon as you get one |

Traits and variants are the long tail of progression. They won't transform a weak roster, but on a strong player they're the difference between a good base and a great one.

### How to redeem codes

1. Launch Blue Lock Farm on the [official Roblox platform](https://www.roblox.com/).
2. Open the **Shop** menu.
3. Scroll to the bottom and find the **Enter code here** field.
4. Paste a code exactly as written and hit Redeem.

Copy and paste rather than typing. One extra space or a swapped letter is enough to make a perfectly valid code fail, and you'll usually only get one attempt.

The game's Discord server is the best source for new codes, patch notes, and milestone rewards. The developer's Roblox group posts announcements too. ## Common Progression Mistakes That Stall Your Farm

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Better habit |
|---|---|---|
| Hoarding Cash | Idle Cash earns nothing | Reinvest at the end of every session |
| Buying lockers before upgrades | Adds cost without multiplying value | Upgrade crate value first |
| Grading with Cash at high tiers | Millions per roll drains your farm | Bank Grade Tokens instead |
| Trickling lockers one at a time | Wastes your Potion windows | Stack lockers, then open in one session |
| Ignoring movement and base size | Idle players mean empty crates | Fix capacity before chasing luck |
| Letting codes expire | Free tokens are free multipliers | Redeem codes the day you see them |

Treat this Blue Lock Farm progression guide as a checklist rather than a one-time read. Run through it at the end of each session and you'll catch the leak before it costs you a day of farming.

## Blue Lock Farm Progression FAQ

**What should I upgrade first in Blue Lock Farm?**
Crate value, then luck, then base size. Crate value multiplies every sale you make, luck determines how strong your roster gets over time, and base size decides how many players are working at once. Opening speed and movement are useful, but they should never come before those three.

**Do I need codes to progress quickly?**
No, but they're the biggest shortcut available. Grade Tokens and Trait Tokens from codes save you millions in Cash grading, and Potions let you squeeze more value out of sessions you were already going to play. Redeem them before you start farming, not after.

**Should I grade with Cash or Grade Tokens?**
Use Cash at low tiers while rolls are cheap, and switch to Grade Tokens once high-tier units start costing millions per roll. Saving tokens for a UR push gives you the biggest Cash multiplier in the game without draining your farm's operating budget.

**How long does it take to reach the late game in Blue Lock Farm?**
It depends on how consistently you reinvest and how often you play. Players who upgrade before expanding and redeem every code tend to move through the early and mid game far faster than players who buy lockers on impulse. The loop is the same for everyone — the difference is discipline.
