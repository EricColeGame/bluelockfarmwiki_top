## What Blue Lock Farm Actually Is (and Why the Loop Matters)

Blue Lock Farm is a Roblox tycoon-style experience from developer Finding Illusions that turns the Blue Lock anime premise into a solo soccer money machine. Instead of competing in matches, you buy lockers, hatch anime-inspired players out of them, and let those players farm soccer balls on your plot. Full crates of balls convert into Cash, and Cash buys the next round of lockers and upgrades. There is no combat and no PvP pressure, so the skill ceiling is really about efficiency: how fast you can hatch, how quickly crates fill, and how much each sale is worth.

That's the short version of Blue Lock Farm how to play. The longer version matters because the game is almost entirely menu-driven. New players often buy a locker, watch a player jog around, and never realize that crates have to be sold manually or that upgrades multiply everything downstream. Your first hour of decisions compounds, so it pays to understand the loop before you start spending.

| Stage | What you do | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buy a locker | Spend Cash at the locker shop | A locker that hatches a player |
| Hatch | Wait for the locker to open | A new farming player |
| Farm | Player automatically collects soccer balls | Balls drop into crates |
| Sell | Sell a full crate at the sell point | Cash |
| Upgrade | Spend Cash on upgrades and better lockers | A faster, more valuable loop |

## Blue Lock Farm How to Play: Controls, Camera, and Interface

Blue Lock Farm uses Roblox's standard control scheme, which means you probably already know 90% of the inputs before you spawn. You walk with the keyboard, look with the mouse, and interact with buttons by clicking them or pressing the prompt key. The developer can rebind keys or add shortcuts at any time, so if something doesn't match the table below, open the in-game settings menu and check.

| Input | Default Roblox binding | What it does in the farm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Move | W, A, S, D or arrow keys | Walk around your plot and the Plaza |
| Look | Mouse movement | Rotate the camera |
| Jump | Spacebar | Hop over crates and props |
| Sprint | Shift (common in Roblox games) | Travel faster between lockers and sell points |
| Interact | Left mouse click or the on-screen prompt | Use stands, buttons, and shop NPCs |
| Camera zoom | Mouse wheel | Pull the camera in or out |
| Close menu | Esc | Exit the Roblox menu; game UIs have their own close button |

Because Blue Lock Farm is a management game rather than an action game, your real "controls" are the interface buttons. Knowing where each one lives saves you minutes of wandering every session.

| Interface element | Where to find it | Why you'll use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shop | On-screen button | Buy lockers and redeem codes |
| Code field | Bottom of the Shop menu | Enter promo codes |
| Ranking stand | Plaza | Grade players with Grade Tokens or Cash |
| Upgrade panel | Near your base | Spend Cash on permanent multipliers |
| Sell point | Beside your crate line | Convert full crates into Cash |

## Step-by-Step: From Your First Locker to Full Crates

If you want a clean start, follow this order. It isn't the only way to play, but it avoids the most common beginner trap: buying lockers you can't feed because your crates never get sold.

| Minute | Priority | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–2 | Walk the plot and find the sell point and upgrade panel | You need to know where Cash enters and leaves |
| 2–4 | Buy the cheapest locker | Starts your first hatching cycle |
| 4–8 | Let the first player farm, then sell the first crate | Confirms the loop is actually working |
| 8–12 | Buy a Crate Value or Luck upgrade | Multiplies every future sale |
| 12–15 | Buy a second locker | Parallel hatching beats one slow locker |

1. Spawn in and orient yourself. Find the sell point, the upgrade panel, and the locker shop before you spend anything.
2. Buy your first locker. Cheaper lockers hatch faster and cost less, which is exactly what you want while Cash is tight.
3. Let the hatched player work. Players farm balls automatically — you don't need to steer them.
4. Sell as soon as a crate fills. Empty crates sitting on your base are wasted income.
5. Reinvest immediately. Every sale should go into either an upgrade or another locker.
6. Expand in parallel. Once you own three or more lockers, opening speed upgrades start paying for themselves.

One tip that experienced players repeat constantly: don't save Cash for a big locker purchase while your crates sit full. Selling early and upgrading often almost always beats hoarding.

## Upgrades, Grade Tokens, and Rankings Explained

Upgrades are where your farm stops being linear. Each one targets a different bottleneck, and buying them in the wrong order is the fastest way to slow your progress.

| Upgrade | What it improves | Buy it when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crate Value | Cash earned per crate sold | Early and often — it multiplies everything |
| Luck | Odds of hatching rarer players | Once Crate Value feels stable |
| Opening Speed | How fast lockers hatch | When you own three or more lockers |
| Movement Speed | How fast your players travel | When your base gets large |
| Base Size | How many lockers and crates fit on your plot | Right before you buy more lockers |

Grade Tokens are the second half of the progression puzzle. You spend them at the **Ranking stand** in the Plaza to grade your players, and grading raises their Cash multiplier. You can also roll grades with Cash, but high-tier rolls reportedly cost millions per attempt, which makes Grade Tokens the smarter path for anyone who isn't already rich. The UR grade is the top prize and carries the biggest Cash multiplier, so it's worth banking tokens until you have a player you actually plan to keep.

| Resource | How you get it | Best use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash | Selling full crates | Lockers and permanent upgrades |
| Grade Tokens | Codes and milestone rewards | Grading players at the Ranking stand |
| Trait Tokens | Codes and rewards | Applying traits to your players |
| Variant Token | Codes and limited rewards | Variant player rolls |
| Luck / Cash / Grade Speed Potions | Codes and rewards | Timed boosts during long grind sessions |
| Jackpot Token | Codes and rewards | A shot at a large bonus payout |

## Common Mistakes That Slow Down New Players

- **Letting crates sit full.** A full crate earns nothing. Sell it and put the Cash to work.
- **Buying the most expensive locker first.** Expensive lockers are slow. You want volume before quality.
- **Ignoring upgrades.** A single Crate Value upgrade often outearns a whole extra locker in the early game.
- **Spending Grade Tokens on throwaway players.** Bank them until you hatch someone worth keeping.
- **Skipping codes.** Free Potions and Tokens are pure profit and take two minutes to claim.
- **Overbuilding before expanding.** Base Size limits how much you can place, so upgrade it before you buy more lockers.

## FAQ

**How do I start Blue Lock Farm how to play on a fresh account?**
Buy the cheapest locker first, let the player farm balls, sell the first full crate, and spend that Cash on a Crate Value upgrade. Repeat that cycle and add lockers as your income grows.

**Do I need to control the players manually?**
No. Players farm balls on their own once hatched. Your job is buying lockers, selling crates, and choosing upgrades — the movement controls are mostly for getting around your plot and the Plaza.

**What are Grade Tokens used for?**
Grade Tokens are spent at the Ranking stand in the Plaza to grade your players. Higher grades mean bigger Cash multipliers, and the UR grade is the strongest one available. Grading with Cash is possible but gets extremely expensive at high tiers.

**How often do Blue Lock Farm codes come out?**
New codes typically appear alongside updates, milestones, and seasonal events. Community sources reported five active codes in late September 2026, but the reliable move is checking the official Discord or the Finding Illusions group after every patch.
