## Why Unit Choice Decides Your Entire Farm

If you want your soccer empire to print Cash, you need to know which players deserve your lockers, your Grade Tokens, and your Potions. Finding the Blue Lock Farm best units is the difference between a base that stalls out in the mid game and one that keeps compounding. This guide breaks down how to judge the Blue Lock Farm best units by rarity, grade, traits, and variants — and the order to spend your resources so you never waste a token on a player you'll replace in an hour.

Blue Lock Farm follows an idle-tycoon loop: buy lockers, hatch players out of them, and let those players farm soccer balls that fill your crates. Selling a full crate converts into Cash, and Cash buys better lockers plus upgrades that raise crate value, luck, opening speed, movement, and base size. A unit's real value isn't just its rarity label — it's how much Cash it helps you generate per hour of play.

### The Five Things That Actually Matter

Before you chase a shiny hatch, run every unit through the same filter. Community reports consistently show that players who evaluate units on output rather than looks progress two to three times faster through the early and mid game.

| Criterion | What It Measures | Why It Matters | Red Flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash output per cycle | Balls farmed × crate value | Directly drives your income | High rarity, low output |
| Drop weight | How rare the hatch is | Determines how many lockers you'll burn | Chasing a 0.1% roll too early |
| Grade tier | Multiplier applied at the Ranking stand | Turns a good unit into a great one | Grading a throwaway unit |
| Traits | Bonus stats layered on top | Free multipliers that stack | Ignoring trait slots entirely |
| Variant status | Alternate version of a unit | Usually a much larger multiplier | Selling a variant by mistake |

The trap most new players fall into is chasing raw rarity. A mid-tier unit with a strong grade and two good traits will out-earn an ungraded showpiece every single time, because the multiplier layers stack on top of each other rather than replacing one another.

## Blue Lock Farm Best Units by Rarity and Grade

Exact tier names and drop rates shift with every major update, so treat the table below as a framework rather than a fixed list. The bands are what matter: each one tells you whether a unit is a stepping stone, a keeper, or fodder.

| Rarity Band | Where It Fits | What To Do With It | Long-Term Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Common / Uncommon | First 10 minutes | Keep the best one, sell the rest | Fodder |
| Rare | Early game | Use until an Epic appears | Temporary |
| Epic | Early-to-mid game | Grade the best two you own | Bridge unit |
| Legendary | Mid-to-late game | Grade fully, add traits | Keeper |
| Mythic / Secret-tier | End game | Everything goes into these | Chase target |

Grade is the second half of the equation, and it's where the current Blue Lock Farm best units separate themselves from the pack. Grading happens at the **Ranking stand** in the Plaza. You can roll grades with Cash, but high-tier rolls cost millions each, which is exactly why Grade Tokens matter so much — they let you chase top grades without draining your bank. The UR grade carries the biggest Cash multiplier in the game, so any unit you plan to keep long term should eventually be pushed there.

| Grade Tier | Typical Cost | Impact on a Keeper Unit | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low grades | Cheap Cash rolls | Small multiplier, fine for early fodder | Skip |
| Mid grades | Moderate Cash | Noticeable bump on mid-tier units | Situational |
| High grades | Millions in Cash or tokens | Strong multiplier, worth it on Legendaries | High |
| UR | Grade Tokens preferred | Largest Cash multiplier available | Top priority |

A useful rule of thumb: never spend Grade Tokens on a unit you'd sell for locker money. Save them for the unit you expect to still be farming with a week from now.

## Traits, Variants, and Tokens: The Hidden Multipliers

Two players with identical names and identical grades can perform very differently once traits and variants enter the picture. Traits are bonus stats attached to a unit, and variants are alternate versions that typically carry a bigger multiplier than the base form. Community reports suggest that a variant with a strong trait set can be worth more than two or three ordinary copies of the same unit — which is why veterans hoard tokens instead of spending them the moment they drop.

| Token or Potion | What It Does | Best Use |
|---|---|---|
| Trait Tokens | Add or reroll traits on a unit | Spend only on keeper units |
| Grade Tokens | Roll grades without Cash | Push keepers toward UR |
| Variant Token | Unlocks or upgrades a variant form | Save for your top unit |
| Luck Potions | Improve hatch odds | Use before a big locker opening session |
| Cash Potions | Boost earnings for a duration | Use during long active sessions |
| Grade Speed Potions | Speed up the grading process | Stack with a grading spree |
| Jackpot Token | Grants a chance at a free reward | Redeem when you have a spare minute |

### Which Traits to Chase First

- **Income traits** — anything that raises Cash or crate value. These pay for themselves fastest.
- **Luck traits** — better odds on future hatches, which compounds into better units.
- **Speed traits** — faster farming cycles mean more crates per hour.
- **Utility traits** — movement and base-related bonuses that smooth out your layout.

If you can only chase one category, chase income. It funds everything else, including the lockers you'll need to find the Blue Lock Farm best units in the first place.

## Upgrade Priority: Where Your Cash Should Go First

Your base upgrades are just as important as your roster. A stacked lineup on an under-upgraded base will always lose to a decent lineup on a maxed one, because upgrades multiply every unit you own at once.

| Priority | Upgrade | Why It Comes First |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Crate value | Multiplies every sale, no exceptions |
| 2 | Luck | Better hatches mean better units, sooner |
| 3 | Opening speed | More lockers opened per session |
| 4 | Movement | Fewer wasted seconds per farming cycle |
| 5 | Base size | Room for more units once income supports it |

The logic is simple: crate value pays for luck, luck produces better units, and better units make opening speed and movement worth investing in. Base size should be the last thing you dump money into — extra slots are worthless if you don't have units worth placing in them yet.

## A Practical Farming Routine for Chasing the Blue Lock Farm Best Units

You don't need to grind for eight hours straight. You need a repeatable routine that stacks your multipliers at the right moments.

| Step | Action | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sell all full crates | Every session start |
| 2 | Reinvest into crate value | Until returns slow down |
| 3 | Pop Luck Potions | Right before opening lockers |
| 4 | Open lockers in bulk | While luck buffs are active |
| 5 | Grade the best hatch | Immediately, using tokens |
| 6 | Apply traits | Only to confirmed keepers |
| 7 | Redeem codes and milestone rewards | Whenever new ones drop |

A few extra habits separate fast accounts from slow ones:

- **Bank your tokens.** Holding Grade, Trait, and Variant Tokens until you hatch something worth keeping prevents the classic mistake of wasting them on early fodder.
- **Watch the community channels.** The game's Discord server and the developer's Roblox group are where update notes and milestone rewards get announced first.
- **Redeem codes promptly.** Limited-time codes hand out Potions and tokens that directly accelerate grading and hatching, and expired codes can't be recovered.
- **Track your own numbers.** Write down what each unit earns per hour before and after grading. Real data beats guesswork every time.

## FAQ

**What are the Blue Lock Farm best units for a brand-new player?**
Early on, the best unit is whichever high-output hatch you can grade cheaply. Don't hold out for a Mythic-tier pull — a graded Epic will carry your first hour of farming and fund the lockers you need to find something better.

**Do I need to spend Robux to get the best units?**
No. The entire progression loop runs on Cash and tokens earned in-game. Luck Potions, Grade Tokens, and milestone rewards from codes all accelerate the process without any purchase.

**How much does grading change a unit's value?**
A lot. Grade multipliers stack with traits and variants, and the top grade delivers the largest Cash multiplier available. That's why Grade Tokens are usually better spent on a unit you plan to keep rather than on a temporary pickup.

**How often do the Blue Lock Farm best units change?**
Every time the developer pushes a balance update or adds new lockers, the ranking shifts. Rather than memorizing a list, learn the evaluation criteria — output, grade, traits, variant — and you'll be able to judge any new unit the day it drops.
