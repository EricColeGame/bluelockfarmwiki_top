## How We Rate Progression in Blue Lock Farm

Every rating below uses the same five criteria, weighted by how much they affect a normal farming session. The point is not to rank systems by how fun they feel — it is to measure how much each one changes your Cash per hour relative to what it costs you in time, Cash, or tokens.

| Rating Criteria | What It Measures | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash impact | How much the system raises crate income per hour | 30% |
| Cost efficiency | Return on Cash or tokens spent | 25% |
| Accessibility | How early a new player can unlock it | 20% |
| Long-term scaling | Whether it still matters at high levels | 15% |
| RNG dependence | How much luck can waste your investment | 10% |

A five-star score does not mean "fun." It means efficient. A system can be the most entertaining part of the game and still score a two if it never pays for itself. Keeping that distinction in mind is what separates players who snowball from players who stall out around mid-game.

## Core Progression Systems: Rated and Ranked

The core loop is simple: buy lockers, hatch players, let those players farm balls, sell full crates for Cash, then pour the earnings back into better lockers. Every other mechanic in the game is essentially a multiplier stacked on top of that loop. According to community reports, the upgrade tracks that matter most are crate value, luck, opening speed, movement speed, and base size.

| System | Progression Rating | Best For | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Locker tier upgrades | 5/5 | Everyone | Directly raises player quality and crate output |
| Grade Tokens | 4.5/5 | Mid-to-late game | Skips expensive Cash rolls for high-tier grades |
| Luck Potions | 4/5 | Farming sessions | Better hatches and rolls during active play |
| Grade Speed Potions | 4/5 | Grinding grades | Compresses the time between grading attempts |
| Cash Potions | 3.5/5 | Short bursts | Strong when stacked, weaker when used casually |
| Base size upgrades | 3/5 | Idle farming | More room for farming players, slower payoff |
| Movement upgrades | 2.5/5 | Quality of life | Convenience more than raw profit |

The pattern here is worth internalizing: anything that multiplies crate value or player quality sits at the top, while anything that only makes the game feel smoother sits near the bottom. Movement upgrades are comfortable, but comfort does not compound.

### Where New Players Usually Go Wrong

Player experience consistently points to the same mistake — spreading Cash across every upgrade track at once. Splitting investment across five systems means none of them reach the threshold where they actually change your income. Pick one multiplier, max what you can, then move on.

## Grade Tokens vs. Cash: Which Ranking Route Is Better?

Grading happens at the Ranking stand in the Plaza, and you have two ways to pay: Cash or Grade Tokens. For low-tier units, Cash rolls are cheap enough to spam. For high-tier units, cash rolls can cost millions per attempt, which makes tokens the far smarter long-term play.

| Route | Typical Cost | Best Used On | Rating |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cash rolls | Low for early grades, millions for high tiers | Fodder units and early progression | 3/5 |
| Grade Token rolls | Free from codes and events | High-tier units chasing UR | 4.5/5 |

The UR grade carries the biggest Cash multiplier in the game, which is exactly why it is so expensive to chase with Cash alone. Saving Grade Tokens for your best units — instead of burning them on early fodder — is one of the highest-leverage decisions a player can make. Community reports suggest treating tokens as a late-game currency, not a convenience item.

### A Simple Token Rule

If a unit is going to be replaced within a day, grade it with Cash. If a unit is going to stay in your lineup for a week or more, spend the tokens. That single rule prevents the most common form of wasted progress.

The order matters more than the individual numbers. Buying speed upgrades before you have anything worth opening is a classic trap, and it is one of the main reasons players plateau. If you want a rule of thumb: never upgrade a multiplier for a system you have not already built.

### Stacking Multipliers Correctly

Potions are temporary multipliers, so timing beats quantity. A Luck Potion used during a long farming session with a fully upgraded base does far more work than the same potion popped while you are reorganizing your lockers. Save potions for sessions where you are actively hatching and selling, and let them expire only when the crates stop flowing.

### How to Redeem Codes

1. Launch the game through the official Roblox website and wait for the lobby to load.
2. Open the Shop menu from the main interface.
3. Scroll to the bottom of the Shop panel to find the code entry field.
4. Paste a code exactly as written — no extra spaces, no capitalization changes.
5. Press Redeem and confirm the reward appears in your inventory.

Copying and pasting directly from a code list is the safest method, since a single wrong character will make a code fail silently. If a code does not work, it has almost certainly expired rather than been entered incorrectly.

## FAQ

**What does a Blue Lock Farm rating actually measure?**

It measures efficiency, not entertainment. Each system is scored on Cash impact, cost efficiency, accessibility, long-term scaling, and RNG dependence, so a high rating means the system reliably converts your time and resources into more income.

**Are Grade Tokens better than Cash for grading players?**

For high-tier units, yes. Cash rolls can run into the millions per attempt at the top end, while Grade Tokens let you chase the UR grade without spending anything. For throwaway units you plan to replace quickly, Cash rolls are perfectly fine.

**Which upgrades should I buy first in Blue Lock Farm?**

Locker quality and crate value first, then luck and opening speed. Base size and movement upgrades are useful but should wait until your income is stable, because they improve comfort rather than raw profit.

**Do Blue Lock Farm codes expire?**

Yes, most do. Codes are typically tied to updates, milestones, or events, so redeem them as soon as you find a working one and check community sources regularly for replacements.
